An SMB signs a €20,000 contract to rebuild its website. The new design is clean, animations are smooth, and management is pleased. Six months later, traffic is flat, the conversion rate gained 0.2 points, and nobody can tell if the investment generated a single euro in profit.

The problem is not the budget. It is the question asked of the vendor. "Redesign our site" reveals nothing about what is actually blocking sales. A €20,000 invoice often corrects a visible symptom rather than the actual leak.

## Two Costly Mistakes

The first mistake is redesigning too early. Companies change the look, feel, and colors while keeping the same checkout funnel, slow pages, and vague value proposition. Clients pay for design rather than revenue growth. Three months post-launch, the same causes produce the same poor results.

The second mistake is optimizing an obsolete tech stack. Teams add plugins to compensate for an ill-suited CMS, pay maintenance fees on unsupported core versions, and patch fragile integrations. Each fix lasts a few weeks. Technical debt accumulates faster than conversion gains.

Between these two extremes lies a high-ROI zone where targeted optimization yields significant returns for minimal cost. Knowing your exact position is key.

## The Audit Before the Proposal

No serious proposal should be signed without a preliminary audit. Order matters:

1. **Analytics:** Where does revenue originate? Which pages convert, and which consume traffic without results?
2. **Session Recordings:** Observing user hesitation, rage clicks, and form abandonment points (with proper user consent).
3. **Search Console:** Keywords gaining ground, declining rankings, and high-impression, low-click pages.
4. **Core Web Vitals:** LCP under 2.5s, INP under 200ms, CLS under 0.1. These thresholds remain core user experience metrics.
5. **Funnel Analysis:** Step-by-step evaluation to pinpoint where drop-offs peak.
6. **Customer Interviews:** Asking recent buyers and lost prospects a simple question: why did you not purchase?

An audit costs a fraction of a redesign and often delivers an uncomfortable conclusion: the site does not need a rebuild.

## The Decision Formula

The formula relies on a single line:
`Monthly revenue loss × payback months = cost of redesign`

Consider a concrete example. A site receives 12,000 visits per month, with 60% on mobile. Mobile conversion sits at 0.9%, while desktop reaches 2.0%. Average order value is €80. If mobile conversion closed even half that gap, it would generate about 40 additional orders monthly, or €3,200. Calculations rely on internal metrics, not generic industry reports.

If a redesign costs €20,000, payback takes over six months, ignoring traffic volatility risks. Alternatively, spending €4,000 on targeted mobile checkout optimizations returning €1,500 monthly achieves payback in under three months.

## When a Redesign is the Right Answer

Certain signals are unequivocal:

- The CMS cannot support required core features, requiring weeks of custom workarounds for basic modifications.
- The tech stack is no longer supported, security updates have ceased, hosting providers threaten deprecation, and qualified developers are scarce.
- Information architecture is the core conversion bottleneck. If visitors cannot locate pricing or core offerings because menus mirror internal corporate departments, a new layer of CSS changes nothing.
- Over 60% of templates require replacement. At this stage, rebuilding is cheaper than patching.

## Securing SEO During a Redesign

A poorly handled migration can wipe out years of organic search equity:

1. Map all legacy URLs and pair each old page with its successor via **301 redirects**.
2. Migrate content along with metadata, titles, and structured schema data.
3. Crawl indexable pages pre- and post-launch to verify response codes and canonical tags.
4. Keep tracking active on launch day.
5. Run comparative analyses at 30, 60, and 90 days to catch organic traffic dips immediately.
